EpiSimulator 🦠

Move one slider. Watch an outbreak change course.

EpiSimulator is a free educational tool designed to teach students about the complexities of epidemic spread and the impact of public health measures. Built with NetLogo and Unity, the simulation lets learners manipulate parameters such as transmission rate, quarantine measures, and vaccination effectiveness, and observe the effects on the spread of a virus in real time.

Within the SMILE program, EpiSimulator is embedded in StudyBuds to ask a question about AI agent roles: does a learner understand an epidemic better after being asked good questions by a peer agent, or after having to correct a teachable agent that holds misconceptions?

Research Questions

  1. How does interacting with an inquiry-driven peer agent versus a teachable agent shape learners' conceptual understanding of epidemic spread?
  2. Does having to identify and correct an agent's misconceptions produce different reasoning about public health measures than being prompted with questions?
  3. How do learners' parameter manipulations in the simulation relate to what they come to understand about transmission, intervention, and non-linear spread?

What Learners Manipulate

Learners change parameters and watch the outbreak respond in real time, making the non-linear relationship between individual measures and population-level outcomes visible.

  • Transmission rateHow readily the virus passes between individuals on contact — the parameter that most sharply demonstrates non-linear growth.
  • Quarantine measuresIsolating infected or exposed individuals, showing how timing and coverage change the shape of the curve.
  • Vaccination effectivenessHow well vaccination protects individuals and, indirectly, the wider population.
Built with NetLogo and Unity. NetLogo provides the agent-based epidemiological model; Unity provides the interactive front end. The simulator is freely available for educational use.

Two AI Agents, Two Ways of Learning

The simulator is embedded in StudyBuds, which allows the same simulation to be paired with different conversational agents. The study compares two contrasting roles:

Condition A

💬 Inquiry-Driven Peer Agent

The agent acts as a curious peer, asking questions that push learners to explain what they are seeing, justify a parameter change, or predict what will happen next.

Here the learner is the one being questioned — the agent's role is to surface reasoning the learner might otherwise leave implicit.

Condition B

🎓 Teachable Agent

The agent holds misconceptions about epidemic spread, and the learner's task is to identify and correct them — learning by teaching, with the agent as the object of critique.

Here the learner is the authority. Correcting someone else's flawed model requires articulating what the right model actually is.

This contrast connects EpiSimulator to the wider SMILE question of what roles generative AI should play in a learning environment — a question also taken up by the misconception correction mode in the Climate Change Simulator and by the Butterfly Tutor in the Flocking Simulator.
